# OrcaGif – Free & Private GIF Maker and Editor

**English** · [Deutsch](README.de.md) · [Français](README.fr.md) · [Español](README.es.md) · [العربية](README.ar.md) · [हिन्दी](README.hi.md) · [中文](README.zh.md) · [বাংলা](README.bn.md) · [Português](README.pt.md) · [Bahasa Indonesia](README.id.md)

Turn a video into a GIF, change a GIF you already have, or save your result as a small WebP or WebM file. OrcaGif is a single HTML file. Everything runs locally in your browser: no upload, no account, no cookies.

## Quick start
1. Open `OrcaGif.html` in your browser.
2. Pick a tab at the top: **🎬 Video to GIF** (make a GIF from a video) or **🎨 GIF Editor** (change a GIF, picture, video, WebP or WebM you already have).
3. Choose the file type (GIF, WebP or WebM), click the blue button and download the result.

> The in-app guide “How it works – quick guide & FAQ” at the top of the page explains the same steps in simple words, in all 10 languages.

## Which file type should I choose?
- **GIF** works everywhere (chats, forums, e-mail), but the files are bigger. Not sure? Choose GIF.
- **WebP** is much smaller than GIF and looks better, and it can have a see-through background. All current browsers show it; some older programs do not.
- **WebM** is the smallest and smoothest, but it is a video: no see-through background, and creating it takes longer than the clip itself (it is recorded in real time – keep the tab in the foreground).
- **Quality** (WebP and WebM): High, Medium or Low. Lower quality means a smaller file.
- If WebP or WebM is greyed out, your browser cannot create that format. Use a current version of Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

## Make a GIF from a video – step by step
1. Click the **🎬 Video to GIF** tab.
2. Click the file button on the left and choose a video from your device (MP4 and WebM work in every modern browser; MKV and HEVC depend on your browser and device). It is not uploaded anywhere, and even files of several GB work.
3. Choose the part you want: drag on the bar under the video, use **Set start here** / **Set end here** (keys `I` / `O`) or type the times. **▶ Play clip** shows a preview. Short clips of 2–6 seconds work best.
4. Set **Output width** (how wide the GIF is; 480 is a good start) and **FPS** (pictures per second; 15 looks smooth). Smaller numbers make a smaller file.
5. Choose the file type and press the blue button. Keep the tab in the foreground until the bar is full.
6. Press the blue download button. For a GIF you can first press **Edit in GIF Editor** to add text.

## Change a GIF you already have
1. Open the **🎨 GIF Editor** tab and choose your file on the left: a GIF, picture, video, **animated WebP** or **WebM**. Drag & drop works too.
2. Press ▶ to play. Move the slider to look at single pictures (frames).
3. Under **Edit**: **Start frame** / **End frame** cut off the beginning or end, **Speed** makes it faster or slower, **Reverse** plays it backwards, **FPS** (max. 50) and **Output width** change smoothness and size, **Crop** cuts the picture.
4. Choose the file type, press the blue button and download the result. Made a mistake? Press **Undo** at the top.

## Add text, pictures and drawings
- **+ Text** puts words on your GIF. Type them in the box on the left, choose font, size and colour, and drag the text to the right place. **Double-click the text in the picture to edit it again** (or double-click it in the layer list).
- **+ Image** adds your own picture, for example a logo. **✏️ Draw** lets you draw with the mouse or your finger.
- Every text, picture or drawing is a **layer**. Click a layer in the list to select it; ▲ ▼ move it up or down, ✕ deletes it. **Visible from frame** / **to frame** decide when it is shown.
- Mouse wheel on a layer = size, Shift + wheel = rotation. Layers can also be dragged outside the image (dimmed margin) and brought back later.

## Make things move
- **Quick way:** select a text or picture and press an effect: Fade in, Fly in, Pulse, Wiggle, Spin or Bounce.
- **Your own way:** tick **Animate (keyframes)**, go to another frame and drag the layer to a new spot. OrcaGif fills in the movement in between. Pick an easing if you like.

## What do these words mean?
- **Frame** – one single picture of an animation. A GIF is many frames shown quickly one after another.
- **FPS** – pictures per second. More is smoother, but the file gets bigger.
- **Output width** – how wide the finished file is, in pixels (tiny dots on the screen).
- **Dithering** – a GIF can only use 256 colours. This trick mixes in tiny dots so that skies and skin look smoother. Switch it on for photos and videos, leave it off for logos and flat drawings. (Only for GIF.)
- **Transparent background** – the see-through parts show what is behind the picture. Works in GIF and WebP.
- **Layer** – a text, picture or drawing that lies on top of your GIF.
- **Keyframe** – a saved position, size or angle at one moment. OrcaGif fills in the movement between two keyframes.
- **Watermark** – the small “OrcaGif” text in a corner. It is on by default – please keep it on to support this free tool.

## My file is too big – what now?
Use a smaller **Output width**, fewer **FPS** or a shorter clip – or choose **WebP**. If the colours look blotchy in a GIF, switch **Dithering** on.
